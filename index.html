Our mission

Help local organizations make sustainability real.

The Sustainable Development Lab is a collaborative, student-led research group that supports local businesses and organizations in implementing sustainable initiatives through experimental research, evidence-based recommendations, and pro bono consulting.

Our vision

A community that learns what works, and keeps doing it.

A community where every local organization, not only the largest ones, can try sustainable ideas, see the evidence, and keep what works. And a campus where undergraduates have a real path into research, community partnership and mentoring each other.

Good intentions aren't enough. Small organizations need evidence.

Large organizations can pilot something, measure it, and drop it if it fails. Independent businesses can't, so they often do nothing.

Most sustainability advice for small businesses is generic, and there is rarely evidence about whether a practice will work in their setting. Meanwhile, student sustainability research at UBC is fragmented and often tied to accredited courses and on-campus partners, which leaves out many students and many community organizations.

  • 1

    Advice is generic

    There's usually no proof a given practice works for a specific organization.

  • 2

    Few can afford to experiment

    Limited capital means trying, measuring and dropping ideas isn't an option.

  • 3

    Research is hard to reach

    Students and community partners outside existing programs often have no way in.

We run the experiment for them.

We compare options directly, so limited budgets go toward what works rather than what sounds reasonable. Partners are co-designers, not research sites.

  1. Consult

    We start by asking what problems the organization really has and what's feasible.

  2. Co-design

    We set the research question together, so the solution is one the partner helped build.

  3. Experiment

    We pilot and compare options, collecting real data over the program.

  4. Report

    Evidence-based recommendations, shared in plain language with partners and the public.

  5. Hand off

    Partners keep going after we leave, and our work is documented for the next team.

Exploring a circular economy for sports equipment in B.C.

Our DEVEC Circular Economy cluster is looking at a simple problem: cost is the most commonly reported barrier to youth sport in Canada, and B.C. has no central hub for equipment donations. Meanwhile, surplus gear sits unused. We're exploring whether a connected system could cut waste and open sport up to more kids.

  • Map who already collects, refurbishes and donates equipment
  • Measure supply, demand and where the friction is
  • Pilot a connection between donors and recipients
